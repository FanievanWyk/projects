# Backup & Disaster Recovery

## Context

A complex operational environment depended on virtual servers, file services, administrative data, cloud platforms, and user devices. Backup existed as a technical function, but resilience required a layered design and clear recovery thinking.

## Challenge

Improve protection against hardware failure, accidental deletion, corruption, site-level events, and service disruption without relying on a single technology or location.

## My role

- Assessed critical services, data locations, dependencies, and recovery risks
- Implemented and operated layered backup capabilities
- Integrated onsite protection, offsite replication, and cloud services
- Considered restore capability and operational continuity alongside backup completion
- Supported documentation, monitoring, troubleshooting, and continual improvement

## Approach

1. Identified which services and data required protection and why.
2. Separated fast local recovery from offsite resilience.
3. Used multiple layers to reduce dependence on a single device, platform, or location.
4. Included Microsoft cloud services in the wider continuity view.
5. Focused on recoverability, not only successful backup jobs.
6. Planned improvements around realistic operational priorities and available resources.

## Outcome

The resulting approach improved resilience through onsite backup, offsite replication, and cloud-supported protection. It also moved continuity planning toward service recovery rather than simple data copying.
