# Enterprise Network Modernisation

## Context

When I joined the organisation, the network was comparatively small: approximately 5–15 switches, with wireless point-to-point links connecting some buildings. As demand grew, the environment needed to move from a limited, organically developed network to a structured campus platform capable of supporting administrative systems, teaching technology, mobile devices, audiovisual services, security systems, and cloud platforms.

I designed the expanded network approach and worked as part of the installation project that delivered more than 500 new UTP data points together with fibre links between key locations. After this foundational project, I continued upgrading and modernising the infrastructure in phases as user numbers, buildings, services, security needs, and technology requirements increased.

The resulting estate grew to more than 50 switches and 100 wireless access points, supporting approximately 1,600 users and 375+ managed computers.

## Challenge

Transform a small network with inter-building wireless links into a structured, scalable campus environment—and then continue modernising it without disrupting daily operations. The solution needed to improve performance, coverage, resilience, and manageability while supporting future expansion and a mixed-vendor estate.

## Constraints

- Limited maintenance windows
- Active users throughout the academic calendar
- Existing buildings, routes, equipment, and evolving requirements
- Budget and procurement cycles
- Multiple technology vendors
- Long replacement lifecycles
- Need for operational continuity during construction and later upgrades

## My role

- Assessed the original network, physical limitations, coverage, and growth requirements
- Designed the expanded network approach and contributed directly to the installation project
- Helped deliver more than 500 UTP data points and new fibre connectivity between key locations
- Defined priorities, dependencies, standards, and phased upgrade outcomes
- Evaluated technical proposals and supplier recommendations
- Coordinated implementation with internal staff, contractors, and external providers
- Managed escalation, testing, documentation, operational handover, and subsequent lifecycle upgrades
- Balanced immediate requirements with long-term reliability, maintainability, and expansion

## Approach

1. Established a practical view of the original topology, building links, capacity, coverage, and failure risks.
2. Replaced dependence on inter-building wireless links with a structured fibre-backed approach where practical.
3. Designed and delivered the foundational cabling expansion, including 500+ new UTP points.
4. Prioritised core infrastructure before visible endpoint improvements.
5. Planned later upgrades in manageable phases aligned to budget and low-risk maintenance windows.
6. Standardised configuration and documentation principles across a mixed-vendor environment.
7. Treated wired networking, wireless coverage, security, server access, audiovisual services, and building projects as one connected system.
8. Included expansion capacity and lifecycle replacement in each design decision.

## Outcome

The work transformed the environment from a small network with limited inter-building connectivity into a structured campus platform supporting more than 50 switches, 100+ wireless access points, approximately 1,600 users, and 375+ managed computers. The initial UTP and fibre project created the physical foundation; continued phased upgrades then supported cloud services, security, classroom technology, administration, audiovisual systems, and future construction.

The programme also replaced isolated, reactive upgrades with a longer-term infrastructure roadmap focused on resilience, supportability, and sustainable growth.
