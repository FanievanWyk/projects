# Enterprise Network Modernisation

## Context

A large education environment had grown over many years, with increasing demand from administrative systems, teaching technology, mobile devices, audiovisual services, security systems, and cloud platforms.

The estate included more than 50 switches and 100 wireless access points supporting approximately 1,600 users and 375+ managed computers.

## Challenge

Modernise the core, distribution, access, and wireless environment without disrupting daily operations. The solution needed to improve performance and manageability while supporting future expansion and a mixed-vendor estate.

## Constraints

- Limited maintenance windows
- Active users throughout the academic calendar
- Existing cabling, fibre routes, buildings, and equipment
- Budget and procurement cycles
- Multiple technology vendors
- Long replacement lifecycles
- Need for operational continuity

## My role

- Assessed the existing environment and recurring operational problems
- Defined priorities, dependencies, standards, and phased outcomes
- Evaluated technical proposals and supplier recommendations
- Coordinated implementation with internal staff and external providers
- Managed escalation, testing, documentation, and operational handover
- Balanced immediate requirements with long-term maintainability

## Approach

1. Established a practical view of the existing topology, capacity, coverage, and failure risks.
2. Prioritised foundational work before visible endpoint improvements.
3. Planned upgrades in manageable phases aligned to budget and low-risk maintenance windows.
4. Standardised configuration and documentation principles across a mixed-vendor environment.
5. Treated wired networking, wireless coverage, security, server access, and building projects as one connected system.
6. Included expansion capacity and lifecycle replacement in each design decision.

## Outcome

The programme created a stronger platform for cloud services, security, classroom technology, administration, and future construction. It also reduced reactive decision-making by replacing isolated upgrades with a multi-year infrastructure roadmap.
